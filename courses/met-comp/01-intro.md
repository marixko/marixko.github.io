## What is Statistics?

Statistics can be regarded as a branch of Mathematics (which historically developed a great deal thanks to Physics!). We can understand it as a field of study that aims to understand the errors associated with our decisions. In other words, it is a field largely built on Probability Theory.

Statistics is also taken to mean all the devices and computations used to understand our data. We can then split Statistics into two:

- **Descriptive statistics:** aims to describe the data through graphs and summary measures.
- **Inferential statistics:** relates the data in order to make inferences about some hypothesis regarding some population.

By the end of the course, you should also get a sense of two philosophical camps: the frequentist and the Bayesian. What I can say now is that in Astronomy it is very common to do Bayesian inference (why?), but keep in mind that frequentist statistics should not be neglected and is also very important.

Much of the progress of Bayesian statistics is due to advances in hardware and computational methods (this will become clearer in the second part of the course!), hence the name of the course: **Computational and Statistical Methods**.

We will cover several descriptive and inferential methods; for the latter, we will cover the frequentist view (first half of the course) and the Bayesian view (second half of the course). All of these terms will become clearer as the course progresses.

Also, the mathematical notation for the topics covered in this course can vary a lot depending on the reference used.
