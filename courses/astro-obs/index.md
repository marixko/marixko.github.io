This Observational Astronomy course was designed for graduate students (master's and doctoral) in Astronomy at Observatório Nacional, as a one-semester subject. The idea is to follow the path of the photon: from the astronomical source, through the atmosphere, the telescope and the detector, all the way to the calibrated measurement that goes into a paper.

The course assumes no prior observing experience, but it does assume familiarity with general physics, a little optics and the basic statistics of data analysis. The first four modules deal with observing in the optical and near-infrared, where most of the techniques were historically established; the last two open up to the rest of the spectrum, to the time domain and to large surveys.

Since a single semester cannot cover any of these topics in full, these notes are meant mainly as a reference to consult later, with the formulas and orders of magnitude that come up in the day-to-day work of reducing and analyzing data.

Corrections and comments are welcome. Enjoy the course!
